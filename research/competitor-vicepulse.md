## Why it works (in order of importance)

1. **The topic is for everyone, not one fan base.** "Is the GTA 6 map empty?" is a question every GTA player has an opinion on. Our titles are about one streamer ("Lacy tried to free the cows"), which only lands with that streamer's fans. YouTube Shorts tests each video on a small, broad audience first; broad topics win that test.
2. **The first sentence is a fight.** "Stop saying X" / "You can't just X" / "Imagine X". It contradicts something the viewer believes, so they stay to see if it's true, and they comment to argue. The top comments are people arguing (2,246 likes on one "half the buildings" comment, 64 replies). Comments and replays push it further.
3. **Super short and dense.** 14-25 s, about 3 words per second of narration, a new shot or zoom every 1.5-2.75 s. Nobody gets bored, and many people watch twice, so "viewed" goes over 100%. Our reactions are 40-70 s with pauses; Instagram data already shows ours drop to 24-27% watched past 45 s.
4. **A narrator tells the story.** A clear, energetic voiceover explains what is happening, so the video makes sense with no context. Our reaction clips need you to know the streamer and catch his mumbled lines.
5. **Big, simple visuals.** Full-screen map or gameplay with a red/green highlight, a big number ("60%", "70%"), a top banner that states the claim. Our stack layout spends half the screen on a facecam/avatar.
6. **Title = the question in the video.** "How Much Of The Map Is Actually Empty?!", "How Many Rockstar Maps Fit Inside GTA 6?!", "How Long It Actually Takes To Drive Across GTA 6?!". Curiosity + "?!" Their top 3 Shorts are all map/scale questions.
7. **Timing.** They published in the 5 days before launch, when the whole internet was searching "GTA 6 map". Since launch they've switched to gameplay stories ("You Can't Just Steal A Plane In GTA 6..." 240K, "Fastest Way To Steal A Military Jet" 119K, "Craziest Random Encounter In GTA 6?!" 53K in a day).
8. **One a day, at US prime time.** Each Short gets a full day to be tested before the next one competes with it.

## What they do that we must NOT copy

- **Fake footage.** The vidIQ breakdowns of their post-launch Shorts say the footage is modded GTA 5 / flight-sim clips presented as GTA 6.
- **Made-up numbers.** "Over 70% enterable buildings" was never confirmed by Rockstar; commenters already call it out.

Our rules stay: real GTA 6 footage only (Twitch streams, credited), verified facts only. That is also our edge: now that the game is out, we have the real thing and they are faking it.

## What we change (action plan)

1. **New main format: "narrated story" Shorts, 15-25 s.** Take the best real stream moment, cut it to the 3-5 shots that tell the story, and put a fast narrator voice over it (vidIQ voiceover, Liam, ~3 words/s), with stream audio low underneath and the streamer's best line left loud at the payoff. The streamer's facecam stays visible (your rule), but smaller, so the game fills most of the screen.
   Example from today: "There's a Bigfoot hiding in GTA 6's swamps... TheBurntPeanut chased it into the water... and the game called it the Skunk Ape."
2. **Hook line first, every time.** First 2 s = narrator line + top banner that challenge or tease: "You can't...", "Stop saying...", "Imagine...", "Nobody noticed...", "GTA 6 actually...". No slow intros.
3. **Broad titles, creator in the description.** Title is about GTA 6 itself ("There's A Bigfoot Hiding In GTA 6?!"), not "Lacy did X". The creator credit stays in the description and caption.
4. **Hard length cap.** Narrated Shorts 15-25 s. Reactions 20-35 s max. No more 45-70 s cuts.
5. **Captions like theirs.** Word-by-word, bold white with black outline, key word in a colour, centre-lower screen; a fixed top banner with the claim.
6. **Debate topics as fact videos.** Verified, on-screen facts that people argue about: map size, how long to drive across, what's enterable, money, police, prices. Still only things we can see in real footage.
7. **YouTube pace: fewer, better.** 3-4 strong Shorts a day on YouTube, spaced through the US afternoon/evening (16:00-23:00 UTC), instead of every 30 minutes. Instagram can stay faster.
8. **Loop the ending.** End on the payoff with no outro so the last line runs into the first.
